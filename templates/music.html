Music, honestly

Name a station — a genre, a language, a mood — and it plays in your voice channel. Nothing to search, nothing to link, and when something goes wrong it says which thing.

{# ------------------------------------------------------------------ #} {# What it does #} {# ------------------------------------------------------------------ #}

Playback

Three complaints, answered

There is nothing to guess about

A station is its address, so there is no search, no match to get wrong and no link to expire. The bot used to resolve songs from media platforms and gave that up: a platform that has decided a datacenter address is a robot cannot be argued with from this side, and "it played a different upload" is what a search that half-worked looks like. Every station here was fetched and listened to before it was written down.

A station that goes quiet is reported, not skipped

A dropped connection reaches the player looking exactly like a track that finished, so the queue used to move on in silence and nobody was told. A live stream has no end, so an ended one is now a card naming the station, how long it played and what the engine said — to the room, and to whoever runs the bot.

ffmpeg does the work, and it says so

A station sends MP3 or AAC; Discord wants Opus, so the bot decodes and re-encodes — at the source's own quality rather than the channel's much lower default, in one command line written in one place. Effects and volume changes re-render the stream and tell you so, and a station that ever serves Opus directly would be passed through untouched instead.

Radio, which needs nobody's permission

🎛️ What is said out loud

That station went quiet. Said with how long it played, because a live stream that ends is a dropped connection rather than a song finishing — one station doing it is that station, all of them is the host.

That station would not open. Said instead of a skip that looks like a bug, and a different card from the one above, because "it stopped" and "it never started" are fixed differently.

No station by that name. Said with the catalogue to browse, rather than silence — the search forgives spelling and understands genres, moods and languages as well as names.

Every music failure is also one entry in the operator's own log — once per reason, not once per track.

The limits

What it will not do

It cannot watch a stream

A Go Live broadcast is not available to a bot: Discord has no endpoint for it and the gateway never carries the video. So the bot can tell you that somebody is live, and it will never pretend to show you a picture of it.

It will not play a song by name

That used to be the headline feature, and it was given up on purpose rather than fixed: resolving a named song means asking a media platform, and a platform that has decided a datacenter address is a robot answers every request with a check instead of a stream. Cookies and proxies paper over it until the next change. A station needs none of that, so the honest thing to offer is a catalogue rather than a search box that fails a third of the time.

It says when it cannot play at all

A host needs an audio decoder and Discord's own encryption library before a single frame can be sent. If either is missing — or if the host simply cannot reach a station from where it runs — every music command answers with what is wrong and how to fix it, instead of joining your channel and playing silence.
