Music, honestly

Search for a song, paste a link, or tune a station. It plays in your voice channel — and when something goes wrong, it says which thing.

{# ------------------------------------------------------------------ #} {# What it does #} {# ------------------------------------------------------------------ #}

Playback

Three complaints, answered

It plays the song you asked for

Every track keeps the provider's own id for that recording, so a link that expires is re-resolved by asking for the same recording rather than searching again — a second search is a second set of results, which is how a bot ends up playing a different upload than the one on its card. When two results are genuinely too close to call, it asks which one you meant instead of guessing.

A cut-off stream is reported, not skipped

A stream that dies reaches the player looking exactly like a track that finished, so the queue used to move on mid-song and nobody was told. A stream that ends far short of its own length now posts a card naming the track, how much of it played, and what the provider claimed.

The audio is the provider's, not a re-encode

It asks for audio-only Opus and passes those packets through untouched whenever they already fit the channel — no decode, no re-encode, no loss. Volume starts at 100% precisely because anything else forfeits that path. Effects and volume changes are the one case that re-renders the stream, and they tell you which of the two happened.

A provider refused this host. Said as that, because it is a fact about where the bot runs and not about your search. An operator can give it cookies or a token to fix it; radio works meanwhile.

Nothing was found. Said with what was searched, rather than a skip that looks like a bug.

Nobody is asked to guess. When the search is ambiguous the bot offers the candidates as buttons.

Every music failure is also one entry in the operator's own log — once per reason, not once per track.

{# ------------------------------------------------------------------ #} {# The limits, which are the point of the page #} {# ------------------------------------------------------------------ #}

The limits

What it will not do

It cannot watch a stream

A Go Live broadcast is not available to a bot: Discord has no endpoint for it and the gateway never carries the video. So the bot can tell you that somebody is live, and it will never pretend to show you a picture of it.

It depends on a platform that would rather not

Playback resolves recordings from third-party services, and those services can refuse a host that looks like a datacenter. That is a fact about the deployment, not a bug in your search, and the bot says so at the moment it happens. The radio stations and the lyrics lookups do not depend on it.

It says when it cannot play at all

A host needs an audio decoder and Discord's own encryption library before a single frame can be sent. If either is missing, every music command answers with what is missing and how to fix it, instead of joining your channel and playing silence.
